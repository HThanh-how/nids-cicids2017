How Optimistic Is Your Intrusion-Detection Benchmark?

Leakage-resistant evaluation and measured serving cost

Huy-Thanh Pham1,2 · Anh Truong*,1,2

1 Ho Chi Minh City University of Technology (HCMUT) · 2 Vietnam National University Ho Chi Minh City · * anhtt@hcmut.edu.vn · SOICT 2026, paper 9222

0.99→0.32

XGBoost attack recall on CICIDS2017 when only the split changes: from a random split to a split that shares no attack family.

1The problem

Learned detectors report macro-F1 above 0.98 on public benchmarks as a matter of routine. Practitioners do not see that performance in the field.

A random split scatters every attack episode across the train/test boundary, so the model is tested on traffic it has already seen. We keep models, hyperparameters and preprocessing fixed, vary only the evaluation, and measure how much of a score belongs to the protocol.

2Fixed pipeline

Standardisation, selection and oversampling are part of the model: re-fitted inside every training split, so nothing leaks from test data.

3Two protocols

Day-disjoint: train Monday–Wednesday, test Thursday–Friday. Every test attack is a family the model never saw.

Is the best model really best?

XGBoost − Random Forest = 0.00044 macro-F1 (5 seeds)

paired t-test p = 0.13 · Wilcoxon p = 0.19 · 95 % interval −0.0002 to 0.0011, which includes zero.

The “best model” claim is unsupported: the data separates two tiers (tree ensembles and the rest), not the winner.

4Recall collapses

silent failure: false-positive rate even improves

XGBoost: recall 0.9935 → 0.3164, macro-F1 0.9879 → 0.6887; Random Forest 0.9937 → 0.3109. Missed attack flows rise from 185 to 36,506. The false-positive rate even improves (0.0073 → 0.0030), so the failure is silent.

5The ranking inverts

Under the day-disjoint split LogReg is first by macro-F1 only by being less selective: FPR 0.0592 against 0.0030 for XGBoost. Threshold-free the order reverses: ROC-AUC 0.6448 against 0.8526. A benchmark that flatters a model can also select the wrong one.

6What drives the gap?

Near-duplicates explain about a hundredth (0.9935 → 0.9821). Benign false-positive rate is 0.0030 on the training days and 0.0033 two days later: no drift. Recall collapses only on unseen families. Leave-one-family-out agrees: botnet 0.0000, port scan 0.0036.

7Precision at real prevalence

XGBoost, measured TPR and FPR carried to the prevalence of a production link. At one attack per thousand flows, the best case (random split) gives precision 0.120: 7,257 false alerts per million flows, seven of eight alerts false.

8Serving cost, measured

The amortised model cost (0.001056 ms/row) would predict 9.5×10⁵ flows/s; the endpoint sustains 404 requests/s before latency collapses, an overstatement of about 2,300×. Killing one of four replicas under load lost 35 of ~8,000 requests and recovered in 3.13 s.

Evaluate your NIDS this way (G1–G8)

G1report a split that shares no attack family beside the random one, and the gap
G2fit scaling, selection and oversampling inside each training split
G3per-family recall with test counts, leave-one-family-out
G4seeds, intervals, paired tests
G5rank at a stated operating point or threshold-free
G6state prevalence and translate error rates to the intended link
G7document feature alignment for cross-dataset claims
G8measure the served endpoint by stage; never convert an amortised model cost into a throughput.

Limits

CICIDS2017 has documented labelling defects, so absolute scores are not field accuracy. Two corpora, four conventional models, one serving host with a co-located load generator. Deep sequence models are open under the same protocol.
